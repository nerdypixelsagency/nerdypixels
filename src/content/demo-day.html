1Event overview

Demo Day runs fully online on Saturday, 31 October 2026, 09:45–17:15 WAT. A produced livestream is broadcast to YouTube and LinkedIn, and registered guests also get access to the voting, games and networking rooms.

Nine teams pitch, NPD launches its new website live, and a panel, speakers and games keep online viewers watching and voting all day. With 19 days to go, the plan relies on fast, organic, community-led promotion rather than paid media.

Goals for the day

Showcase the teams

Give all 9 teams a professional broadcast stage to present their product and launch strategy to judges, brands and recruiters.

Launch the website

Launch the new NPD website with a moment people share, and drive its first wave of traffic.

Grow NPD Academy

Position NPD as the place where African digital marketers learn by building, and turn viewers into Cohort 2 enrolments.

3Pitch format and judging

Four judges score every team out of 100 on a shared online scoresheet, with marketing strategy carrying the most weight. People's Choice is a separate viewer award, so the main result cannot be swung by whichever team brings the biggest online crowd.

Judging panel (4 seats, all remote)

Brand marketer

A senior brand or growth marketer from a recognised African brand.

Founder or investor

A founder or early-stage investor.

Product lead

A product or technology lead.

NPD faculty

Chief judge and tie-breaker.

The virtual format lets us invite judges from anywhere in Africa or the diaspora. Each judge receives a webcam-and-headset checklist and joins the 28 October rehearsal.

Scoring rubric

CriterionWeightWhat judges look for
Marketing and launch strategy30Clear audience, positioning, channel mix, budget, 90-day plan, measurable KPIs
Product quality and live demo20Working MVP, core action completed live on screen, polished and trustworthy interface
Problem and customer insight15Real African problem, evidence the customer cares
Creativity of the launch idea15A launch moment people would talk about and share
Business model and feasibility10How it makes money or saves cost; realistic next steps
Pitch delivery10Clarity, on-camera presence, time discipline, handling of Q&A

Scores are entered in a locked Google Form or sheet during each Q&A and collated by the programme lead during deliberation.

Timekeeping rules

  • An on-screen countdown timer overlay is visible to the team and to viewers.
  • The producer shows chat cues at 5, 2 and 1 minute; at 15:30 the team's audio is faded and the MC steps in.
  • Overruns cost 2 rubric points per 30 seconds.
  • Every team submits by 27 October a 3-minute backup demo video and a full pre-recorded pitch, played if their power or internet fails.

People's Choice voting

  • Run on Slido or Mentimeter, gated by registration: one vote per registered email, checked against the attendee list.
  • Voting opens at 15:20 and closes at 15:50, after all nine teams have pitched, so early teams are not disadvantaged.
  • Each project gets a page in the online showcase gallery on the new website, so viewers can review every team before voting.

Keeping the online room alive

  • One link and QR code for games and votes, shown as a permanent on-screen overlay and pinned in every platform's chat.
  • A dedicated chat moderator on each streaming platform welcomes people, answers questions and passes the best comments to the MC.
  • The MC reads viewer names and locations on air ("greetings to Abeokuta, Accra and London"), which makes people stay and invite others.
  • Polls every 20–30 minutes, such as "which launch channel would you use?", with results shown on stream.
  • A live WhatsApp community for registered viewers gets segment alerts, such as "Website launch in 10 minutes".
  • Audience prizes delivered digitally: data bundles, airtime, NPD course discounts and one full Cohort 2 scholarship.

9Recommendations: duration, attendance and impact

Keep the main event to one day, inside a virtual "Demo Week". A single six-hour broadcast on the 31st keeps the focus sharp. Short online sessions before and after it build momentum and extend the reach.

Online, extra days cost little, but more than about six hours of live viewing in one day causes heavy drop-off.

DayFormatWhat happens
Wed 28 OctLivestream, 1 hourPublic pitch-order draw streamed live; teams share 60-second teasers
Fri 30 OctLivestream, 90 minutes"Demo Day Eve" masterclass with a judge or speaker; showcase gallery previews the 9 projects
Sat 31 OctLivestream, full dayDemo Day as planned above
Mon 2 NovLivestream, 1 hourWinners' AMA and Cohort 2 information session

Alternative: if viewer fatigue is a concern, split Demo Day into two half-days. Pitches would run on Friday 30 October (afternoon), with the website launch, panel and awards on Saturday 31 October. This works only if the 31st stays the headline day.

Drive attendance

  1. Let teams sell the event: People's Choice and the referral leaderboard give all nine teams a reason to bring their networks online.
  2. Make the live moments worth catching: tease the website launch and awards, and give audience prizes only to people watching live.
  3. Use daily content, not one announcement: the nine-day team spotlight keeps the event visible every day until 26 October.
  4. Recruit through partners: each judge, speaker and sponsor shares once, and community leaders get named on-air shout-outs.
  5. Go beyond Lagos: a virtual event can reach students and professionals across Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya and the diaspora, so promote it in those communities too.
  6. Invite hiring managers: "meet job-ready digital marketers" gives brands and agencies a reason to join the networking rooms.
